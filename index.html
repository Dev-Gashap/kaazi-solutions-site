03 / Project delivery

Build the infrastructure behind AI.

KAazi helps organizations take AI infrastructure projects from requirements through deployment and operational readiness — coordinating the vendors, the site and the sequence, and handing over infrastructure that is documented and ready to run.

Strategy → Requirements → Infrastructure planning → Vendor coordination → Deployment → Commissioning → Operational handover → KAazi platform
01 / Scope

Define what the infrastructure has to do.

Workload profile, compute requirements, capacity targets, and the power, cooling, network and storage envelope the site has to meet. The output is a requirements set a vendor can quote against and a plan a board can approve.

02 / Coordinate

Run the project across the parties involved.

OEMs, integrators, colocation providers, remote hands and internal teams. KAazi manages the program and the sequence; physical execution is delivered by qualified partners, customer teams or the OEM, whichever fits the site.

03 / Hand over

Commission, document, and onboard.

Validation against the original requirements, as-built documentation, and operational handover — with assets onboarded into the KAazi platform so the record starts on day one rather than being reconstructed later.

10 / FAQ

Questions worth asking up front.

What is KAazi?

An AI compute infrastructure company with two connected businesses. The first is AI infrastructure project delivery — helping organizations plan, coordinate, deploy and commission the infrastructure AI compute runs on. The second is the KAazi platform, which is being built to manage those compute assets, their capacity, health, maintenance and lifecycle once they are running.

Does KAazi sell GPU compute today?

No. KAazi does not own or operate compute infrastructure, and there is no capacity to purchase. Leased and KAazi-operated compute appear on the long-term roadmap, and we will say so plainly when that changes.

Where is the platform today?

In development. Asset, capacity and health intelligence are the first capabilities being built; maintenance operations, lifecycle management and economics follow. The dashboard shown on this page is a product mockup with illustrative figures, not a live customer environment. We are talking to design partners.

What is the Compute Passport?

A persistent digital operational record for an individual compute asset — its identity, serial and UUID, where it sits down to the rack and slot, its health history, utilization, incidents, maintenance, warranty and RMA history, operating hours and lifecycle status. The idea is that an accelerator's history travels with it, instead of living in a spreadsheet somebody left behind. It is a product concept in development.

Does KAazi replace NVIDIA Mission Control or Run:ai?

No. Those schedule and orchestrate workloads on compute. KAazi is not a GPU scheduler and does not run your jobs. It is the system of record for the infrastructure itself — what exists, where, in what condition, and through what lifecycle. It is designed to sit alongside your scheduler, not in front of it.

Is this a DCIM, BMS or CMDB replacement?

No. DCIM and BMS manage the facility; a CMDB manages IT configuration broadly. KAazi is focused specifically on AI compute — accelerator-level assets, usable capacity and lifecycle — and is designed to integrate with those systems rather than replace them.

Does KAazi provide physical deployment?

KAazi coordinates, manages and oversees deployment. Physical execution is delivered through qualified partners — OEMs, integrators, colocation remote-hands teams, or the customer's own staff — chosen for the site. Licensed engineering disciplines are delivered by appropriately qualified firms, not performed in-house.

Can KAazi work with our existing infrastructure vendors?

Yes, and that is the intent. KAazi is vendor-neutral and does not resell hardware. We coordinate across whichever OEMs, integrators and service providers you already work with, and the platform is being designed to read from the systems you already run.

Does KAazi support private and on-premise infrastructure?

That is the primary case. The platform is being designed for private clusters, colocation and on-premise environments, with cloud GPU environments included so a mixed estate can be seen in one place.

What types of infrastructure can KAazi manage?

AI compute estates: accelerators, servers, racks, clusters and sites, along with the operational context around them — capacity state, health, incidents, maintenance, warranty and lifecycle. NVIDIA accelerators are the initial focus, with other vendors on the roadmap.

How does KAazi approach security?

The platform is being designed to read infrastructure telemetry and asset state, not customer workloads, model weights or data. Least-privilege, read-only integration wherever possible, with customer-controlled scope. Detailed security documentation will be published alongside the first release rather than claimed in advance.
